### Recording the voiceover
- Record on your phone in a small room or closet with soft things around you (clothes, blankets). It kills echo.
- Phone about a hand-span from your mouth, slightly to the side.
- Read the whole script 3 times, then keep the calmest take.
- Music under the voice should be quiet, about a quarter of the voice's loudness.

## Caption

My anxious mind is always scanning for danger. So on this artist date, I gave it a different job: find the beauty in things nobody stops for.

A bird, completely unbothered.
A path that didn't ask me to hurry.
Flowers growing whether anyone notices or not.
A colour I didn't have a name for.
The city, still going, while I slowed down.

Julia Cameron writes in The Artist's Way that the reward for attention is always healing. I'm starting to believe her. Noticing small things is one of the few ways I come back to my body when my thoughts get loud.

Your turn: on your next walk, find one ordinary thing that is quietly beautiful. Tell me what it was in the comments.

## Signature style: Artist Date (Hollywood cinematic)

Keep these the same in every artist date video so people recognise it instantly:

1. **The grade:** your warm, slightly faded cinematic colour. Same filter or preset every time.
2. **The frame:** export **vertical 9:16**. For the film look, put the picture in a 4:5 window with thin black bars top and bottom, instead of a wide letterbox (wide bars shrink the picture to a thin strip on phones). Shoot vertical, or horizontal in 4K so you can crop.
3. **The voice:** first-person narrator, slow and warm, always with subtitles (most people watch on mute).
4. **The opening:** a moving shot plus one line about the anxious mind. Never a black frame or title card first.
5. **The closing:** the same end card every time: "Artist Date · the_unrushed.whispers", plus one small invitation to the viewer.
6. **The music:** the same soft instrumental mood (piano or strings) every time.
7. **Length:** 30 to 60 seconds.
